## Who we are

Sherpas Wealth is the first AI Growth Platform for financial advisors ([sherpaswealth.com](https://sherpaswealth.com)). Advisors are our customers. Families are their clients.

For every new client, an advisor does three jobs:

| | Job | Today |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Collect the data**: who the family is, what they earn, own and owe, and what they want | Solved by Sherpas: our **Intake** agent reads any document and fills in the client |
| 2 | **Do the math**: turn that data into a plan, with clear recommendations | Solved by Sherpas: **Paraplanner** and **Portfolio analyst** do the analysis. 8 hours of work becomes 10 minutes per client, so advisors can reach more families |
| 3 | **Explain the plan**: walk the family through it and answer their doubts | **Still done by hand, one meeting at a time.** This is your challenge |

## The challenge

**Bring the plan to life.** Take a family from their plan to understanding it and wanting to act on it: like having an advisor in the room, or better.

Design for either situation: families who will meet an advisor later (they arrive already understanding their plan), or families who never will (the plan has to land on its own).

What success looks like:

- **It captivates.** It grabs their attention in seconds, and holds it.
- **They get it.** It works for someone who knows nothing about finance, and for someone who knows a lot.
- **Made for them.** It feels personal and relevant to this family, and to each person in it.
- **They act.** They want to take the first step, and keep coming back until they do.

Any format, any channel, any device. Sherpas already has guided tours and a chat on the plan: if your idea is only one of those, it scores low on creativity. Use them as a starting point and go further.

**Bonus, the days after:** people think it over, forget, and come back with new questions on day 3 or day 30. If your idea covers those days too, even better.

### How families receive advice today

The family shares their goals and financial information. Sherpas produces an online plan with an overview, recommendations and projections. Days or weeks later, the advisor usually explains it in a screen-sharing meeting. Advisors would rather spend that time on the family's questions, fears and decisions.

Even a clear plan is a lot to take in alone. Partners may want different levels of detail or different ways of learning. After the meeting, families often need time and follow-up before acting. Your experience can help before, during or after that conversation.

### Where families get stuck today

Problems we see when families receive their plan. They are starting points, not solutions: pick one, combine several, or bring your own.

**Understanding**

1. They cannot see how a choice today changes their life years from now: Michael Carter does not know if buying a home in 2027 still lets him retire at 58.
2. Big numbers and percentages do not tell them whether things are good or bad: "$2.37 million left at the end of the plan, in today's dollars" means little to Timothy and Melanie Weah.
3. A good score can hide a serious problem: Samantha Lewis scores 82, "strong", yet her savings are projected to run out at 73.
4. The plan answers what it can calculate, not the worry that keeps them up at night, which nobody has asked them about.

**Attention**

5. They will not sit down and read a long plan: the Weahs, with two jobs and children aged 6 and 3, get to it in short bursts, often on a phone.

**Emotion**

6. Hard news makes people look away instead of act: Michael Carter's plan opens with a score of 26, in the "critical" band.
7. Topics like death and old age get pushed aside: Daniel Johnson holds $250,000 of life cover against a recommended $1.6 million or more, with two children at home.

**Couples**

8. One plan, two people: Timothy is comfortable with more risk than Melanie, and nothing happens until both agree.

**Timing and follow-up**

9. Questions come when nobody is there to answer, and a week after the meeting most of it is forgotten.
10. The plan arrives on its own date, not at the moments when the family would act: Samantha has college bills each August and a home purchase in 2030.

**Action**

11. Too many good options and no first step: the Weahs have spare cash, an 18% card, missing life cover and nothing saved for college, and the plan leaves the order to them. Even once they choose, they do not know how to do it, and nothing reminds them or shows them it worked.

### 4. Read the cases from your code

Call `list_cases` first to get the available case IDs. `get_case` lets you progressively open a case:

| Tool | Arguments | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| `list_cases` | `{}` | Case IDs, households, locations, scores and available parts |
| `get_case` | `{"caseId":"michael-carter"}` | The case overview, available sections and their sizes |
| `get_case` | `{"caseId":"michael-carter","part":"financial-health","section":"narrative"}` | The financial-health analysis text |
| `get_case` | `{"caseId":"michael-carter","part":"financial-health","section":"score"}` | The score and its explanation |
| `get_case` | `{"caseId":"michael-carter","part":"investment-analysis","section":"narrative"}` | The investment-analysis text |
| `get_case` | `{"caseId":"michael-carter","part":"persona"}` | The family's prospect persona, as Markdown text |
| `get_case` | `{"caseId":"michael-carter","part":"persona","section":"D4"}` | One persona section, here risk orientation. Use the heading or its label (`D0` to `D6`, `Transition alerts`, `Subjective concerns`, `Objective concerns`, `Tensions`) |

Omit `section` to fetch a whole part. Parts can contain tens of thousands of tokens, so select the sections your experience needs. The MCP serves each family's three files: `financial-health` and `investment-analysis` as JSON, and `persona` as Markdown text (read it as text; it is not JSON). PDF reports are only in this repository.

**From your code**, send `MCP_TOKEN` from your `.env` as the `Authorization: Bearer <token>` header with an MCP SDK. It lasts 72 hours: a token taken at the kick-off stops on Sunday 15 Nov, so log in again for a fresh one, and take a new one on the morning of demo day. If your MVP must keep working after you hand it in, read the JSON files in `cases/` instead; they never expire.

Python (`pip install mcp python-dotenv`):

```python
import asyncio, json, os
from dotenv import load_dotenv
from mcp import ClientSession
from mcp.client.streamable_http import create_mcp_http_client, streamable_http_client

load_dotenv()

async def main():
    http = create_mcp_http_client(headers={"Authorization": f"Bearer {os.environ['MCP_TOKEN']}"})
    async with streamable_http_client("https://mcp-hackathon.sherpaswealth.com/mcp", http_client=http) as (read, write, *_):
        async with ClientSession(read, write) as mcp:
            await mcp.initialize()
            r = await mcp.call_tool("get_case", {"caseId": "michael-carter", "part": "financial-health", "section": "narrative"})
            print(json.loads(r.content[0].text)["value"]["summary_main"])

asyncio.run(main())
```

JavaScript (`npm install @modelcontextprotocol/sdk`; save as `app.mjs`, run `node --env-file=.env app.mjs`):

```js
import { Client } from "@modelcontextprotocol/sdk/client/index.js";
import { StreamableHTTPClientTransport } from "@modelcontextprotocol/sdk/client/streamableHttp.js";

const mcp = new Client({ name: "my-team", version: "1.0.0" });
await mcp.connect(new StreamableHTTPClientTransport(new URL("https://mcp-hackathon.sherpaswealth.com/mcp"), {
  requestInit: { headers: { Authorization: `Bearer ${process.env.MCP_TOKEN}` } },
}));
const r = await mcp.callTool({ name: "get_case", arguments: { caseId: "michael-carter", part: "financial-health", section: "narrative" } });
console.log(JSON.parse(r.content[0].text).value.summary_main);
```

The proxy speaks the OpenAI API: use any OpenAI SDK with the proxy address and your team key. Model names and prices are in [the table below](#models-and-usage-prices).


Each request uses a small amount of your team's credits.

**Put the two together.** The proxy does not fetch the case for you: your backend fetches a case section through the MCP (or reads its JSON file here), puts it in the model's messages, and asks the model to explain the **existing plan**. For example, in Python (`pip install mcp openai python-dotenv`):

```python
import asyncio, json, os
from dotenv import load_dotenv
from mcp import ClientSession
from mcp.client.streamable_http import create_mcp_http_client, streamable_http_client
from openai import OpenAI

load_dotenv()

async def get_section(case_id, part, section):
    http = create_mcp_http_client(headers={"Authorization": f"Bearer {os.environ['MCP_TOKEN']}"})
    async with streamable_http_client(os.environ["MCP_URL"], http_client=http) as (read, write, *_):
        async with ClientSession(read, write) as mcp:
            await mcp.initialize()
            r = await mcp.call_tool("get_case", {"caseId": case_id, "part": part, "section": section})
            return json.loads(r.content[0].text)["value"]

plan = asyncio.run(get_section("michael-carter", "financial-health", "narrative"))

llm = OpenAI(base_url="https://llm-hackathon.sherpaswealth.com/v1", api_key=os.environ["TEAM_KEY"])
reply = llm.chat.completions.create(
    model="gpt-5-mini",
    messages=[
        {"role": "system", "content": "You explain this family's existing financial plan in plain words. Use only the plan below and do not invent numbers.\n\n" + json.dumps(plan)},
        {"role": "user", "content": "What should I do first, and why?"},
    ],
)
print(reply.choices[0].message.content)
```

### 6. Add voice if your idea uses it

Fastest way to try it: run [`examples/voice/`](examples/voice/) (Node 22 or later and Chrome, about two minutes). It reads its own `.env` inside `examples/voice/`: paste the same block from your access page there. An open voice session counts as one of your team's 4 concurrent requests.

To build your own:

1. Open a WebSocket **from your backend** to `wss://llm-hackathon.sherpaswealth.com/v1/realtime?model=gpt-realtime-2.1-mini`, with `Authorization: Bearer <TEAM_KEY>` in the connection headers.
2. Send a `session.update` event with `session: { type: "realtime", instructions: "..." }`. Include the relevant case narrative in those instructions.
3. Your frontend captures microphone audio and sends it to your backend. Relay it as base64 PCM16 at 24 kHz in `input_audio_buffer.append` events. Audio returned in `response.output_audio.delta` uses the same format; forward it to your frontend for playback.
4. Use headphones for the first test. Ask about the selected plan, then interrupt and ask a follow-up. Stop queued playback when the response is interrupted.
5. Close the WebSocket when finished. The same endpoint and protocol also work with `gpt-realtime-2.1` and `gemini-3.8-live`; change the model name. `gpt-live-1` uses a separate protocol and route, listed below.

Browser audio usually needs conversion to the required PCM format. A WebSocket connection alone is not a complete audio interface. Route voice through your backend and the proxy; direct provider connections do not use the team's hackathon credits.

- **429 Too many requests:** keep within 60 requests per minute and 4 concurrent requests or sessions per team. During the kick-off, if the login page refuses you, wait a few minutes or use your phone's data connection.
- **A key or token leaked** (pushed to GitHub, shown in a video): tell a mentor on Discord and we replace it.
- **An empty `gpt-5-mini` answer:** omit `max_tokens` or use at least 1,000; a very low limit can be consumed by reasoning.

When asking for help, share the step, model name and error message. Do not share keys, tokens or authorisation headers.

- **Text:** OpenAI-compatible `POST /v1/chat/completions` at the proxy address.
- **Realtime voice:** WebSocket `/v1/realtime?model=MODEL_NAME`, for the Realtime models and Gemini Live.
- **GPT Live:** WebSocket `/v1/live/sessions`, with a different protocol from Realtime.
- **ElevenLabs speech:** OpenAI-compatible `POST /v1/audio/speech` (for example `client.audio.speech.create(...)` in the OpenAI SDK). `voice` takes an OpenAI voice name (`alloy`, `echo`…) or any ElevenLabs `voice_id`.
- **ElevenLabs transcription:** OpenAI-compatible `POST /v1/audio/transcriptions` with `scribe-v2`.
- **ElevenLabs budget:** ElevenLabs is shared by every team, so **$1.40 of your team's $50 can go to ElevenLabs** (speech and `scribe-v2` transcription together): about 40 minutes of `eleven-flash-v2.5` speech, or 20 of the other two. When it runs out, ElevenLabs calls are refused and every other model keeps working. Prefer `eleven-flash-v2.5`: it costs half as much and is the least likely to be busy.
- **Team limits:** 60 requests per minute and 4 concurrent requests or sessions.
- **Budget:** the proxy records spend in USD. Your issued team key sets the spending allowance. Check usage through the authenticated `/key/info` endpoint, which returns `info.spend` and `info.max_budget`.
- **Voice sessions:** close them when finished. Voice usage is recorded when a session closes, so a long session can exceed the remaining allowance before the limit takes effect.

## A few financial terms

- **Net worth:** what the family owns minus what it owes.
- **Cash flow:** money coming in minus money going out.
- **Liquidity:** money available to use quickly.
- **401(k) and IRA:** US retirement savings accounts.
- **Roth:** an account funded with after-tax money, with tax-free qualifying withdrawals.
- **529:** a US education savings account with tax advantages.
- **Living map:** a year-by-year view of the family's projected financial life.

**Tech terms**

- **MCP:** Model Context Protocol, a standard way for AI assistants and code to read data from a server.
- **AI proxy:** one address in front of many AI models; your team key pays for the calls.
- **Backend:** the part of your app that runs on a computer or server, where keys stay private.
- **Token / key:** a secret that proves who you are. Your MCP token is personal; the AI key is per team.
- **`.env`:** a private file with your keys, loaded by your code and never committed.
